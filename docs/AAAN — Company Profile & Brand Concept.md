## 2. Our story, leadership and values

### Our founding story

AAAN began in 2015 with three graduates and one shared frustration. Africa grows some of the world's most valuable crops, yet its farmers earn the least from them.

Aaron Appiagyei, an agricultural scientist, had watched farmers plant tired, diseased seedlings and lose much of each harvest before it reached a buyer. Margaret Paula, a food and nutrition specialist, saw the other side: families with too little safe, nutritious food, and raw crops leaving the continent cheaply only to return as expensive processed goods. Antwi Kingsley, a technologist, believed farmers could be connected to knowledge, inputs and markets the way the rest of the economy was being connected.

Together they asked a simple question: what if one network could fix every link, from the seedling to the shelf? They started with a nursery and a group of smallholder farmers in Nyandarua. There they supplied clean potato seed and vegetable seedlings, trained farmers in better methods, and guaranteed to buy what they grew. Yields rose, losses fell and incomes followed.

The model worked, so they took it west. In Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, the world's cocoa heartland, AAAN brought the same approach to cocoa, cashew, oil palm, yams, cassava and plantains, and added processing so more value stayed in Africa. That is why the company is a **network** and not just a farm: every farmer, nursery, processor and buyer in it makes the others stronger.

Today AAAN works across three countries and supplies buyers on every continent. Its founding belief has not changed: **African agriculture, advanced, can feed Africa and the world.**

AAAN's first farmer group in Nyandarua had 120 members. Eleven years later it is a network of more than 25,000 farmers in three countries, and its produce reaches 32 countries.

### Vision, mission and values

**Vision:** An Africa that feeds itself and supplies the world with safe, high-quality food.

**Mission:** To advance African agriculture by giving farmers better seedlings, modern technology and fair markets. We add value to what Africa grows, so more of the wealth from each harvest stays on the continent.

## 3. Brand positioning

AAAN should read as a continental institution, not a local farm business. It is a network that spans two regions, nine core crops and the full value chain.

**Positioning statement:** For buyers, partners and farmers who need reliable, safe African produce at scale, AAAN is the pan-African agribusiness network that controls quality from seedling to export. Unlike traders who only buy and sell, AAAN improves what is grown, how it is grown and what it becomes.

**How the site shows AAAN's scale**

- **Multi-country footprint:** East Africa plus West Africa, shown on a map on the home page.
- **The whole value chain:** seedlings, farming, processing, export. Most competitors do one link.
- **Numbers on the home page:** farmers, hectares, tonnes, countries, export destinations **(see Key numbers in section 1)**.
- **Standards and partners:** certifications, government and research partners, buyer logos **(see sections 7 and 9)**.
- **Professional imagery:** real farms, nurseries, processing lines and people, not stock photos where possible.

**Tone of voice:** confident, warm, expert. Short sentences, concrete facts, African pride without exaggeration. Avoid "small-scale", "trying to" or "hope to".

**Tagline**

Recommended: **"From African Soil to Global Tables."** In French: **"Du sol africain aux tables du monde."**

It carries the whole story in six words: rooted in Africa (soil), the full value chain (soil to table), and global reach (tables of the world). It works on a website hero, a shipping container or a trade-fair banner, and it translates cleanly into French.

Supporting lines for campaigns and section headers:

- *Advancing Africa's Harvest* (corporate and investor material)
- *Seed to Shelf. Africa to the World.* (export and buyer pages)
- *Better Seeds. Better Harvests. Better Lives.* (farmer network pages)

## 4. What AAAN does

AAAN works across the whole agricultural value chain, not one link of it. That is the core of its "global company" story: it controls quality from the seedling to the buyer.

&#91;embedded content: AAAN value chain · 5 stages\]

Value addition is highlighted because it is where AAAN earns more per tonne and keeps jobs in Africa.

1. **Seedlings and nurseries:** certified, high-yield, disease-free planting material for farmers.
2. **Farming and technology:** modern methods on AAAN farms and partner farms: irrigation, soil science, organic practice.
3. **Harvest and aggregation:** collecting, grading, storing and moving produce with minimal loss.
4. **Value addition:** processing raw crops into cocoa products, cashew kernels, palm oil, flours, juices and snacks.
5. **Trade and export:** supplying international buyers and local markets with consistent, traceable produce.
6. **Farmer network:** training, inputs and guaranteed off-take for smallholders. This is the "Network" in AAAN's name.

## 7. Technology, seedlings and safe production

AAAN's advantage starts before planting: better seedlings plus better methods mean bigger, cleaner harvests. This is how AAAN helps farmers grow enough for local tables and for export.

**Improved seedlings and planting material**

- Certified, disease-free seedlings from AAAN nurseries **(12 nurseries in Kenya, Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, producing 5 million+ seedlings a year)**
- High-yield, climate-resilient varieties: hybrid cocoa, grafted cashew, clean cassava cuttings, tissue-culture bananas and plantains, improved yam seed
- Vegetable seedlings raised in controlled nurseries for uniform, strong transplants

**New technologies on the farm**

- Drip irrigation and greenhouses for vegetables
- Soil testing and precise fertiliser use
- Integrated pest management, which cuts chemical use
- Mobile advisory and digital farm records for traceability
- Post-harvest handling: solar drying, cold storage and grading to reduce losses

**Organic and safe food**

- Organic and low-residue production methods, with compost and biological pest control
- Full traceability: every lot can be traced back to the farm, the farmer group and the harvest date
- Lab testing for pesticide residues and aflatoxin, which matters most for cashew and maize

**AAAN certifications and standards**

AAAN's farms, farmer groups and processing plants meet the standards that international buyers require. On the site, these go on a "Quality and Certifications" page and in the footer.

## 9. Impact and partnerships

Impact is what turns AAAN from a supplier into an institution, and it is what governments, development partners and investors look for first.

- **Farmers:** 25,000+ farmers in the network, 42% of them women. Better seedlings, training and a guaranteed buyer have raised their yields by 45% on average.
- **Food security:** 120,000+ tonnes of produce handled each year, including potatoes, milk, cassava, yams, maize, plantains and vegetables for local tables. Drying, cold storage and processing cut post-harvest losses.
- **Jobs:** 850+ direct employees and 4,000+ seasonal and contract jobs each year in nurseries, processing plants and logistics, with a focus on women and youth.
- **Value stays in Africa:** cocoa, cashew, palm oil, cassava and dairy processed in AAAN's seven facilities instead of shipped out raw.
- **Environment:** organic methods, soil health, deforestation-free cocoa and palm oil with farm-level geolocation, and efficient water use.

These can map to the UN Sustainable Development Goals (zero hunger, decent work, responsible production, climate action). Many international partners recognise that framing.
